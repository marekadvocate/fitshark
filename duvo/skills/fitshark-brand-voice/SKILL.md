# Fitshark — Brand Voice & Trust Standards

Apply this to every customer-facing message (reply, follow-up, quote, campaign) so Fitshark always
sounds like one professional, trustworthy team.

## Voice
- **Professional, warm, confident, concise.** A knowledgeable parts specialist who's genuinely helpful.
- Plain language; short sentences and short paragraphs. No walls of text, no hype, no exclamation spam.
- Customer-first: lead with the answer (price + delivery), then the easy next step (order link).
- Honest: "available on order" — never imply in-stock when it isn't.

## Formatting standards
- Greeting: "Hi there," (neutral; use the name only if reliably known).
- Key facts (price incl. VAT, delivery) as a tiny labelled list, never buried in prose.
- One clear primary CTA (the order link/button). Secondary links are smaller/plain.
- Sign-off: "Best regards," then "Fitshark Customer Care".
- Always state "Price incl. VAT" and, on quotes, a validity date.
- Match the customer's language (EN/SK/DE/…); keep product names, prices and links unchanged.

## Trust & fitment lines (include where relevant)
- **Fitment guarantee:** when a vehicle was named and the part matches it, add one line:
  "✓ Confirmed to fit your <vehicle> — or we'll make it right." When fitment is uncertain, do NOT claim
  a guarantee; instead invite them to confirm their exact model/VIN.
- **Reassurance:** "Genuine/quality-brand parts" (only if true from the data), and easy ordering by link
  or reply.
- Keep these to one short line each — credible, not salesy.

## Glossary (use consistently)
- "available on order" (not "out of stock" as a dead end), "estimated delivery", "incl. VAT",
  "your <vehicle>", "order link". Avoid jargon and internal terms.

## Hard don'ts (never, in any customer-facing text)
- Never expose internal data: supplier company names, SKU/part codes, wholesale prices, margins, feeds.
- Never invent prices, lead times, stock, or compatibility.
- Never overpromise delivery or guarantee fitment you can't verify.
- No pushy or repetitive upselling — at most a few genuinely relevant suggestions.
